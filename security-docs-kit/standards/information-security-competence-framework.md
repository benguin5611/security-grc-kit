# Information security competence framework

> Part of the [security-docs-kit](../README.md#before-you-rely-on-anything-here), a starting point for a lean security/GRC function rather than a certified or audit-ready control out of the box. Read that disclaimer and this artefact's own "Adapt this to your context" section before relying on it.

A 0-4 skill-level rubric and a required-competencies-by-role matrix mapping general security domains to a desired level per role. The competency areas follow the 14 control domains of ISO/IEC 27001:2013 Annex A; the 2022 edition regrouped Annex A into four themes, so treat the domain list as a skills taxonomy rather than a map of the current edition's controls.

## 1. Assess competence requirements by role

Roles with minor, general responsibility for the ISMS (end users, individual contributors, team managers with no dedicated security duties) don't need a specific competence-assessment questionnaire; they complete general security-awareness training instead. Reserve formal competence assessment for roles that materially manage or improve the ISMS.

Identify the key competencies and desired level for each of those roles, using the level definitions and role matrix below. Update the list whenever technology, organisational structure, or responsibilities change.

## 2. Assess existing competence levels

- Distribute a questionnaire per role covering all competencies required for that role, without revealing the required level (so self-assessment isn't anchored to the target).
- Ask staff to complete it as objectively as possible.
- Have responses validated by another party where the role warrants it.
- Discuss discrepancies with the individual; where disagreement remains, management decides which level to record.
- Close out once responses are received and agreed.

## 3. Establish competence development actions

Review the gap between required and current competence for each individual. Where a gap exists, raise a corrective action (see [Corrective Action Management](../processes/corrective-action-process.md)) and choose a development approach proportionate to available resources, budget, and timeframe.

## 4. Evaluate effectiveness

Review development actions during performance reviews and regular ISMS management reviews. After a development action completes, assess whether the individual now meets the required level; if not, identify why and take further action. Document everything: training records, mentoring logs, third-party engagement.

## Appendix A: Competence level definitions

| Level | Guidance |
| --- | --- |
| **0** | No knowledge or experience; not part of the role. |
| **1** | Practised infrequently, largely by observation; little understanding of *why*, possibly short tenure in the area; no formal training; a general awareness. |
| **2** | Regularly practised as part of the role, for long enough (typically 1+ year) to feel comfortable doing it; some informal or formal training; understands the principles; feels competent. |
| **3** | A particular strength, backed by significant training, qualifications, and experience (typically 3+ years); fully understands the principles, stays current, may have trained others and shaped processes and procedures. |
| **4** | Recognised subject-matter expert who contributes to developments in this area; held in high regard by peers; may design, develop, or test new products, services, or applications relating to this competency. |

## Adapt this to your context

- **Size**: a solo practitioner is every role at once. Assess yourself honestly against the Security/GRC lead matrix above (it's the closest match) and use any gaps to prioritise your own development or to make an evidenced case for additional headcount.
- **Certification-audit evidence**: this framework, run honestly, is itself a credible artefact to show your ISO 27001 certification body's auditors or an interviewer; it demonstrates you think about competence as a managed, evidenced thing rather than an assumption.
- **The role matrix is a worked example rather than a fixed taxonomy**: the actual roles in your organisation, and which competencies matter for each, will differ. Keep the *shape* (levels 0-4, a matrix per role, a gap-driven development action) and replace the specifics.

**Frameworks referenced**: ISO/IEC 27001:2013 Annex A (the competency areas above follow its 14 control domains; the current 2022 edition regroups Annex A into four themes with 93 controls).
