# ISMS change request & monitoring/measurement

> Part of the [security-docs-kit](../README.md#before-you-rely-on-anything-here), a starting point for a lean security/GRC function rather than a certified or audit-ready control out of the box. Read that disclaimer and this artefact's own "Adapt this to your context" section before relying on it.

The ISMS change-request control and the monitoring/measurement/analysis/evaluation procedure: the machinery behind a management system's continual-improvement clauses.

## Part 1: ISMS change request

Anyone who wants to propose a change to a policy, plan, standard, process, procedure, register, or reference should be able to do so through a documented, low-friction path:

1. **Submit the request**: via whatever intake channel you use (a form, a ticket, a documented email address), including a clear, concise description of the proposed change and any supporting rationale.
2. **Assessment**: the security/GRC function evaluates the request: feasibility, impact analysis, compliance implications, and alignment with good practice. Material changes should follow your change management process, proportionate to their risk, so they're reviewed, documented, approved, and communicated in a controlled way.
3. **Decision and notification**: the requester is told the outcome. If approved, the change is incorporated into the relevant document. If not approved, the reasons are communicated, along with any alternative suggestions.

## Part 2: Monitoring, measurement, analysis, and evaluation

This procedure keeps the ISMS reviewed and improved based on real performance data rather than assumption.

1. **Determine what to monitor**: identify the key processes, controls, and performance indicators that need monitoring to assess ISMS effectiveness, and define the specific metrics and data points for each.
2. **Choose methods**: select monitoring/measurement/analysis/evaluation methods and tools capable of producing comparable, reproducible results, so the measurements are actually valid over time.
3. **Set a schedule**: establish a monitoring/measurement cadence based on the criticality of each process or control.
4. **Assign roles**: define who performs the monitoring and who's responsible for overseeing and coordinating it; make sure they have the skills to do it properly.
5. **Analyse and evaluate**: decide when results get analysed, and establish a process for spotting trends, patterns, and areas of concern or improvement; name who's responsible for the analysis.
6. **Document and communicate results**: retain evidence of monitoring/measurement outcomes (meeting minutes, reports); apply appropriate retention and integrity controls; communicate results to the relevant governance bodies (your security steering function, leadership, board, whatever exists at your scale).
7. **Evaluate overall ISMS performance**: at least annually, based on the analysed metrics, identifying improvement areas and potential corrective or preventive actions.
8. **Review the procedure itself**: at least annually, and improve it based on lessons learned and changing needs.

## Adapt this to your context

- **Size**: a solo practitioner is the requester, the assessor, and the approver for most changes. The value of writing the request down anyway is the same as everywhere else in this kit; it's what makes the decision traceable later, and what stops "I meant to change that" from silently not happening.
- **Governance bodies**: "your security steering function, leadership, board" is deliberately vague; map it to whatever governance layers actually exist for you, from "nobody, it's just me" up to a full steering committee and board.
- **Compliance program**: if you're certifying against a specific framework, that framework's clauses on management review, monitoring, and continual improvement (e.g. ISO 27001 clauses 9-10) set minimum expectations beyond what's described here; check the current requirements.

**Frameworks referenced**: ISO/IEC 27001 (the clauses on monitoring, measurement, analysis, evaluation, and continual improvement this procedure is designed to satisfy).
